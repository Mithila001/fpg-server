#### Chapter 3.2.3 From Geometric Synthesis to Hint-Based Exploration

The first optimization stage in the floor plan generation pipeline produces spatial hints rather than final room geometry. Its purpose is to search for a favorable arrangement of candidate locations that the constraint-based solver can later interpret as soft guidance. In this role, the optimization engine acts as a probabilistic search mechanism over a high-dimensional layout space, where each trial proposes a possible configuration of point-based hints inside the floor boundary. The solver is not bypassed; instead, it is reserved for the more promising candidates identified during exploration.

This design arose from a practical constraint. Directly optimizing full room dimensions through the solver required substantial time per trial, because each attempt combined generation, post-processing, and scoring into an expensive cycle. Under that regime, only a limited number of trials could be executed within the available runtime budget, which was insufficient for stable learning. The revised strategy therefore shifts the optimization target away from exact room construction and toward the generation of hint points. By reducing the cost of each trial, the system can explore a much larger number of candidates within the same time envelope.

The resulting workflow may be understood as a two-level search. At the first level, the sampler proposes a set of coordinates for the relevant spatial entities. These coordinates are not fixed placements; rather, they represent preferred regions from which the downstream solver may infer room arrangement. At the second level, the solver evaluates whether the hinted configuration can be realized under the geometric and relational constraints of the project. This separation between exploratory sampling and exact generation makes the process significantly more efficient, because the expensive solving step is applied only after a candidate has already demonstrated structural promise.

#### Search Space Parametrization and Discretization

A key characteristic of the search space is its reduced resolution. Instead of searching continuously over every possible coordinate, the sampler operates on a discretized lattice defined by a fixed step size. If the floor extent is denoted by $W$ and $H$, and the grid spacing by $\Delta$, then candidate positions are constrained to values of the form $x = x_0 + k\Delta$ and $y = y_0 + m\Delta$, where $k$ and $m$ are integers. This scaling-down strategy lowers the number of admissible states, helps the search revisit meaningful regions, and ensures that sampled hints remain separated by a practical distance. It also aligns the sampler with the granularity expected by the solver.

The sampling parameters are therefore best understood as spatial bounds, grid resolution, and hint radius. The spatial bounds define the admissible region within the floor envelope, the grid resolution determines how finely the space is partitioned, and the hint radius establishes the local neighborhood around each proposed point. Together, these parameters shape the search landscape so that the optimization engine focuses on broad structural relations rather than excessive geometric precision.

Each trial returns a quantitative evaluation of the sampled hint arrangement. In the first stage, this evaluation is based on the internal scoring logic that measures how well the proposed hint map supports a plausible layout. The score is continuous rather than binary, which allows the sampler to distinguish between weak, moderate, and strong candidates and to adapt toward more promising regions over successive trials.

#### Evaluation Workflow and Procedural Refinement

When a trial exceeds the solver gate, the sampled hints are passed into the solver as soft guidance. If the solver successfully constructs a valid floor plan, the result is combined with the earlier sampling score to produce a composite assessment. If the solver does not succeed, the trial still contributes to the search history through the sampling score. This preserves information even from partial failures.

The sampler is used in a focused manner. Its role is not to exhaustively model every architectural rule, but to learn from previous candidate quality and bias subsequent trials toward more useful point patterns. The coarse lattice also introduces a form of spatial regularization by preventing redundant trials that differ only by negligible offsets. From a procedural perspective, each trial proceeds as: propose a coordinate set, evaluate it against structural heuristics, and, if promising, pass the corresponding hints to the solver for exact realization.

Persistent storage is disabled by default in the current workflow, which is consistent with the exploratory nature of the optimization stage. The present design emphasizes the practical interplay between coarse spatial sampling, gate-based solver invocation, and score-driven refinement.

#### Summary

In summary, the optimization stage bridges abstract spatial exploration and exact constraint-based generation. By transforming the problem from full geometry synthesis into hint discovery, the system achieves a larger trial budget, a more informative learning signal, and a better match between exploratory search and solver execution. The low-resolution search space, continuous scoring, and selective solver invocation together form a coherent methodology for producing floor plan candidates that are computationally tractable and structurally meaningful.
