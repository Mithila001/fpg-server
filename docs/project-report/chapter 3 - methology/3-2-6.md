# Chapter 3.2.6: Post-Processing Refinement Stage

## 3.2.6.1 Overview

The post-processing stage is a critical layer between raw floor plan generation and final quality assessment. Its purpose is to convert geometrically plausible but imperfect layouts into plans that are operationally usable, visually coherent, and stable for later evaluation. Generated layouts may satisfy the main structural requirements of the model while still containing local irregularities such as fragmented circulation zones, misaligned boundaries, narrow geometric artifacts, or exterior interfaces that do not align cleanly with adjacent spaces. Rather than forcing the generation model to resolve every local detail directly, the refinement stage applies controlled corrective transformations that improve the plan without changing its spatial logic.

This approach prevents the generator from becoming overly rigid. If every local geometric detail were enforced by increasingly strict constraints, the system could lose solution diversity, encounter avoidable infeasibility, or produce layouts that satisfy abstract rules but remain weak in practical usability. The refinement stage therefore acts as a balancing mechanism: it preserves the global intent of the generated plan while correcting localized defects that would otherwise reduce output quality. It is a conservative geometric repair pipeline rather than a reconstruction step.

## 3.2.6.2 Extended Wall Refinement

Extended wall refinement allows selected room boundaries to grow beyond their initial rectangular footprint when expansion improves geometric continuity or aligns the room better with neighboring spaces. Many generated plans begin as simplified orthogonal blocks that are valid at the constraint level but too rigid for realistic architectural composition. By selectively extending a wall segment, the system can create richer room outlines and reduce the limitations of strictly rectangular layouts.

The process identifies candidate wall segments that can be expanded without disturbing the broader organization of the floor plan. Expansion is not applied uniformly; it is governed by a room-type hierarchy and bounded configuration parameters. The hierarchy assigns priority to certain spatial categories, while the configuration limits the number of rooms eligible for expansion, the number of candidate wall selections, the proportion of a wall that may be affected, and the maximum extrusion distance.

This bounded approach is essential. Unconstrained wall extension could distort the plan or create large-scale geometric changes that undermine the original solution. Instead, only local opportunities for improvement are evaluated. When a candidate is selected, the boundary is extended outward as a geometric patch and merged with the existing room shape, producing a refined polygon that remains compatible with the original plan while gaining additional spatial depth or better adjacency relations.

## 3.2.6.3 Hallway Union

Hallway union addresses a common weakness in automated floor plan generation: circulation space can become fragmented into multiple adjacent segments that are logically part of the same passage but geometrically represented as separate rooms. This fragmentation reduces clarity, complicates scoring, and can create an artificial impression of complexity. The union process resolves this by merging hallway regions that share a sufficiently long overlapping boundary.

The procedure uses a geometric compatibility test. Hallway polygons are treated as closed spatial regions, and their boundary intersections are examined. When the shared boundary length exceeds a minimum overlap threshold, the regions are considered part of the same circulation component and are merged into a single hallway entity. This is similar to clustering, where individual hallway segments are grouped into a larger connected unit when their physical relationship indicates continuity.

The benefit is substantial. A unified circulation zone is easier to interpret, more faithful to architectural convention, and less likely to be penalized by later scoring. It also reduces redundant internal partitions and improves downstream geometric operations because a continuous corridor is more stable than several narrow, nearly touching pieces.

## 3.2.6.4 Snap Floor Plan to Grid

Grid snapping improves geometric consistency by aligning room coordinates to a uniform lattice. Even when the overall shape is correct, coordinate values may contain long decimal fractions caused by earlier geometric operations such as union, extrusion, or intersection. These values are not necessarily wrong, but they introduce numerical noise and reduce stability in subsequent processing. Snapping converts each boundary coordinate to the nearest grid-aligned value.

Mathematically, the transformation is expressed as $x' = g \cdot \mathrm{round}(x / g)$ and $y' = g \cdot \mathrm{round}(y / g)$, where $g$ is the chosen grid spacing. This compresses floating-point variation into a regular spatial structure, making the plan easier to read, easier to compare, and less vulnerable to small discrepancies that can accumulate across stages.

The adjustment remains negligible at the human scale. It removes long decimal tails and precision drift without changing room dimensions in an architectural sense. Grid snapping is therefore a numerical regularization step, not a redesign step.

## 3.2.6.5 Veranda Adjustment

The veranda adjustment stage focuses on the consistency of the exterior frontage, especially where a veranda interacts with an outdoor transition space. Exterior appendages are sensitive to local alignment because even a small mismatch can make the frontage appear disjointed or ambiguous. The refinement process checks whether the veranda and its adjoining outdoor region satisfy a direct adjacency relationship before applying any modification.

When the spaces are properly aligned, the exterior boundary of the veranda can be repositioned so that it conforms more naturally to the width of the adjacent outdoor zone. This improves frontage continuity, reduces awkward projections, and helps the veranda read as an intentional transitional element. If the required adjacency is not present, the system avoids making an arbitrary correction.

## 3.2.6.6 Wall Union

Wall union consolidates overlapping or redundant boundary segments into a unified wall representation. This is especially useful after earlier transformations have modified the floor plan geometry. As room shapes become more complex, multiple polygons may share edges, partially overlap, or generate duplicate boundary traces. Without consolidation, these overlaps can produce redundant wall data and distort measurements such as total wall length.

The union process extracts the boundary structure of the refined rooms and merges all overlapping linework into a single geometric network. Only unique wall segments are retained. The result is a consistent wall structure that more accurately reflects the true architectural perimeter and internal separations of the plan.

This improves scoring, avoids double-counting shared walls, and provides a stable foundation for downstream operations such as opening placement, circulation analysis, and boundary validation.

## 3.2.6.7 Overall Evaluation of the Refinement Pipeline

Taken together, these procedures form a layered correction strategy. Extended wall refinement improves local room geometry. Hallway union reduces circulation fragmentation. Grid snapping regularizes coordinates and suppresses precision drift. Veranda adjustment strengthens exterior coherence. Wall union consolidates the resulting geometry into a reliable structural representation. Each step addresses a distinct class of defect, yet all serve the same goal: transforming a technically feasible floor plan into a higher-quality architectural candidate.

The value of this pipeline lies in its balance between flexibility and control. It is powerful enough to correct small defects and enrich the geometry, but still constrained enough to preserve the original generation logic. The goal is not to replace constraint-based generation with unrestricted editing, but to supplement it with a disciplined repair mechanism that improves usability without sacrificing structural validity.
