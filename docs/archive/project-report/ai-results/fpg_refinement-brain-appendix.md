# Appendix: The Centralized Intelligence of the Floor Plan Generator

The floor plan generator is best understood as a centralized decision system rather than a collection of isolated routines. At its core, a single geometric engine receives normalized requirements, prepares the room set, introduces system-level spatial elements, and then evaluates the design under a unified constraint structure. In this sense, the core acts as the project’s intellectual center: it interprets the program, applies architectural rules, and produces a candidate layout through one coherent optimization framework.
Its strength lies in profile-based governance. The same engine can operate in different modes because the constraint landscape is not fixed; it is composed through declarative profiles. The synthesis profile prioritizes admissibility and broad search freedom, allowing the system to construct a new baseline layout from requirements and spatial hints. The refinement profiles reuse an existing arrangement and shift the emphasis toward local improvement, preserving the overall composition while encouraging better spatial economy, stronger façade coherence, and improved room relations. An additional extender-oriented variant can activate further rules when an extension-specific configuration is required. This design makes the system extensible: new profiles can be added by recombining constraint sets without rewriting the central engine.
The control panel is the mechanism that translates architectural intent into solver behavior. It functions as a governance layer that distinguishes hard guardrails from soft preferences. Hard rules protect structural validity: rooms must remain geometrically consistent, non-overlapping, sufficiently connected, and compatible with key placement and coverage requirements. Soft rules, by contrast, shape quality without invalidating the plan. They guide compactness, dead-space reduction, proximity to the center, façade regularity, and the preservation of useful alignments. The result is a layered judgment system in which feasibility is guaranteed first, and refinement is optimized second.
The system flow is therefore staged. Requirements are first validated and normalized, then the appropriate profile is selected, and the core constructs a seed-informed or seed-free solve depending on the phase. After solving, the layout passes through post-processing and scoring, while progress signals keep the orchestration layer informed about intermediate outcomes, best candidates, and termination states. This makes the generator not merely an optimizer, but a controlled architectural workflow with clear guardrails, adaptive profiles, and observable decision flow.
