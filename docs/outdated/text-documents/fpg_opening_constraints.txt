Opening Constraint Guide
=========================

This document explains the door and window placement rules used in the opening solver. It is written for non-developers and keeps the explanation short and accessible.

Back Door Placement
- What it does: Finds suitable spots for a back door on eligible rooms, such as the kitchen or hallway. It prefers walls that face the rear of the house and selects the best available exterior edge.
- Why it matters: It ensures the back door is placed in a practical location that connects interior circulation with an exterior exit.

Internal Door Placement
- What it does: Determines which rooms may connect with internal doors and limits how many doors each room can have.
- Why it matters: It keeps the interior layout logical by allowing only sensible room pairings, such as bedrooms connecting to hallways, and prevents too many doors from appearing in one room.

Main Door to Outside
- What it does: Chooses a single exterior wall for the main entrance and centers the front door on that wall.
- Why it matters: It guarantees a clear, valid main entrance location and prefers the most appropriate exterior side when multiple options exist.

Windows Placement
- What it does: Selects one window position for each eligible room, using available exterior walls while avoiding clashes with doors.
- Why it matters: It ensures each room with window potential gets a valid opening without conflicting with existing doors, supporting natural light and exterior access.
