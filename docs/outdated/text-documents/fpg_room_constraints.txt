Floor Plan Constraint Guide
===========================

This document explains the room layout constraints used by the floor plan solver. It is written for non-developers and focuses on what each rule does for the floor plan, not on implementation details.

Hard Constraints
----------------

Basic Geometry Constraints
- What it does: Ensures every room has valid shape and size, and that rooms do not overlap each other.
- Why it matters: It creates the basic feasibility of the plan by making sure rooms fit together without collisions and that each room keeps realistic proportions.

Hallway Constraints
- What it does: Makes hallways use a narrow, corridor-like shape, forces them to connect to the main living area and at least one other room, and requires enough shared wall length with adjacent rooms.
- Why it matters: It keeps circulation spaces functional and prevents isolated or awkward hallway placements.

Room Shared Wall Constraints
- What it does: Controls how much wall each room should share with other rooms, depending on the room type.
- Why it matters: It helps create sensible adjacencies and privacy, such as bedrooms being more enclosed and living spaces sharing longer walls with adjacent rooms.

Hard Room Adjacency Constraints
- What it does: Enforces mandatory adjacency rules, such as rooms that must be next to each other or groups of rooms where at least one contact is required.
- Why it matters: It preserves essential functional relationships, for example keeping kitchens near dining areas or bathrooms near bedrooms.

Minimum Area Coverage
- What it does: Requires the total room area to cover a minimum portion of the floor plan.
- Why it matters: It prevents layouts that leave too much empty unused space inside the building footprint.

Room Size Hierarchy
- What it does: Keeps room sizes in proportion by making some rooms relative to the size of the main living room.
- Why it matters: It avoids extreme size imbalances, such as a tiny bedroom next to a huge living room, and keeps the plan feel balanced.

Anchor Room Location Constraint
- What it does: Uses the front-facing room (veranda or living room) as an anchor and places other rooms deeper into the building behind it.
- Why it matters: It maintains a natural front-to-back order in the plan and stabilizes the layout facing the street or entrance.

Veranda Placement Constraints
- What it does: Positions the veranda at the front edge of the plan, ensures it attaches properly to the building, and creates a supporting outdoor space area.
- Why it matters: It secures the veranda’s front-facing placement and creates a realistic outdoor interface that does not overlap with interior rooms.

Garage Placement Constraints
- What it does: Anchors the garage to either the left or right side of the building frontage and ensures it has proper access to the front.
- Why it matters: It makes garage placement practical and keeps it connected to the entrance or driveway areas.

Open Area Placement (Legacy)
- What it does: Describes an older approach to combining veranda and garage frontage rules.
- Why it matters: It is not actively used in the current system, but remains as a historical reference for how frontage behavior was previously handled.

Envelope Staircase Constraints
- What it does: Enforces stepped setbacks along the building exterior, creating a perimeter that keeps rooms from all lining up flush with the outer boundary.
- Why it matters: It shapes the building form and exterior appearance, creating a more articulated facade instead of a flat box.

Soft Constraints
----------------

Seed Layout Hints With Wiggle
- What it does: Guides the solver toward a starting layout using a seed plan, while allowing rooms to move a little from those positions.
- Why it matters: It helps produce results closer to an existing reference layout and speeds up the search for a good solution.

Soft Room Adjacency Preference
- What it does: Marks certain room adjacencies as desirable rather than mandatory.
- Why it matters: It encourages more natural room groupings without strictly forcing them, giving the solver flexibility.

Compact Layout Center Proximity
- What it does: Encourages rooms to stay near the center of the plan and not drift too far to the sides.
- Why it matters: It produces tighter, more efficient layouts with less wasted space.

Bathroom Location Preference
- What it does: Encourages bathrooms to be placed deeper inside the plan rather than near the front.
- Why it matters: It improves privacy and places bathrooms away from the entrance or street-facing side.

Layout Dead Space Penalty
- What it does: Penalizes empty space inside the overall room envelope.
- Why it matters: It pushes the layout to pack rooms together more efficiently.

Seed Facade Depth Penalty
- What it does: Penalizes rooms that sit too far back from the intended facade line based on a seed layout.
- Why it matters: It preserves the intended front appearance and keeps the facade more consistent.

Seed Facade Alignment Penalty
- What it does: Encourages room edges to stay aligned with the seed layout for related rooms.
- Why it matters: It helps maintain a coherent and visually pleasing facade rhythm.

Recessed Facade Penalty
- What it does: Penalizes rooms that are recessed too far behind the main facade line and encourages nearby facade rooms to stay aligned.
- Why it matters: It prevents deep unwanted recesses in the building frontage and keeps the exterior massing clean.

Room Shared Wall Refine Penalty
- What it does: Adds a soft penalty for rooms that fall short of their preferred shared-wall expectations.
- Why it matters: It refines wall-sharing quality without making the layout impossible to solve, improving overall adjacency quality.
